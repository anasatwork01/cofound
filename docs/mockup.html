The design system SPEC §3.1 refers to. Tokens are extracted from this file into packages/ui/src/tokens/; the contrast figures below are computed, and tests/console/contrast.test.ts asserts them on every build.

Authored 2026-09-12 for task L.5. The audience is a semi-technical founder watching an agent work on their product and approving the things that spend money — which is SPEC §21 decision 9 answered as the specification itself assumed it.

How these values were built

The three state hues are fixed by SPEC §18 — violet is an agent-owned action, amber is waiting on you, teal is live — and the clause that actually constrains the design is the one after them: they are “one learned pattern, not three”, used identically for an ads approval, an incomplete Stripe onboarding and a DNS-pending domain.

So the three were built by anchoring contrast, not lightness. Each solid is placed at 4.6:1 against grey-100, the darkest surface any text sits on, and its lightness is allowed to fall wherever that puts it. Pinning lightness instead is the obvious move and it does not work: it is the same number in a space that does not predict contrast, and the three drift apart by the only measure that matters.

What is equalised violet amber teal spread
Contrast anchored at 4.6:1 on grey-100 — this system 6.29 6.30 6.34 0.05
Lightness pinned to three decimal places — rejected drifts 0.90

Near-white ink on each of the three solids. A 0.05 spread is “one learned pattern” in its only measurable form. If a state colour ever has to move — someone dislikes the amber, a hue gets rotated — re-anchor to 4.6:1 against grey-100 and let lightness fall where it falls. Never re-pin lightness.

Colour

A single green-grey ramp at H≈131 carries every surface. It is not a neutral grey and not a blue-grey: the faint green reads as daylight rather than as screen. There is no #ffffff in the system — the page ground is deliberately mid-light so that three luminance planes can carry the whole hierarchy with no box-shadow anywhere, and ink lands at 11.5:1 rather than the 19:1 that halates across a long session.

The tints are at L 95.4–95.5% so they read as a band on both the page and a raised panel (1.08–1.10:1) while still carrying their own solid as text at 5.7:1. That is what lets a tinted row band work anywhere rather than on raised surfaces only.

Type

Atkinson Hyperlegible Next and Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono, one superfamily in two voices. The reason is the reading task rather than a mood: a founder scans amounts, durations, IDs and file paths at speed, and confusing 1 l I or 0 O here costs real money. Atkinson is the typeface engineered specifically to disambiguate those, and it is humanist enough to keep a mid-grey instrument panel from reading cold. Drawing both voices from one family means a sentence with an ID in it reads as one line rather than a paste.

Base 15px, ratio 1.125, seven contiguous steps. Four weights and no more — 400 body, 500 UI labels, 600 panel titles and the gauge readout, 700 page headings — because hierarchy here is weight and position rather than size.

Mono is the voice of measurement and never of labels. Credits, costs, durations, versions, positions and SHAs — anything you compare down a column. Not captions, not eyebrows, not prose. That rule also delivers tabular figures by construction. Italic has exactly one job: marking a value as provisional, such as an estimated cost shown before a turn runs.

The three states

Shape as well as hue, because meaning may never be carried by colour alone — and because the same three marks appear in the project rail, where they are learned before they are needed.

Agent is building Waiting on you Live

Escalation is by scope, not by kind. §18's three examples differ in how much of the screen they concern: a DNS-pending domain is a fact, an ads approval is a row, an incomplete Stripe onboarding is a region. So one vocabulary, three scales.

Google Ads wants a $40 daily budget
Stripe needs two more details
Payouts start once Stripe has your bank details and a business address.

Precedence, decided once and applied everywhere: amber before violet before teal, one marker per row. A version that is both live and waiting on you shows amber, because the thing you can act on wins.

The credit gauge

The one place this interface spends any boldness, because SPEC §18 asks for it by name: build and runtime as separate bars with the active hold drawn as hatching, in the top bar on every screen, “because users need to see burn while causing it”.

Only runtime is graduated, and that asymmetry is the argument: §16.3 gives the two meters different exhaustion behaviours. Running out of build credit pauses the builder, which is harmless because you are at the keyboard. Running out of runtime credit reaches your app's own customers, so it follows a policy ladder — and only it has a threshold worth watching approach. The 80% mark is drawn from a full balance in neutral grey, so you learn where the warning lives before you arrive at it.

A hold is open

The hatched band is credit the agent has claimed for a turn that has not finished. It is violet in both bars — a hold is the agent's claim wherever it appears, so it must look the same in both meters or “hold” is two patterns rather than one — and it is drawn on unspent track only, never over spent fill.

Credits aren't metered yet. They start when your first app goes live.

The instrument is drawn and the reading is absent. Not zero — zero is a lie that reads as “you are out of credits” — and not a spinner, because nothing is loading; the meter does not exist yet. Assistive technology is told the same thing: the progressbar omits aria-valuenow entirely rather than reporting nought. Every slot is reserved in every state, so the top bar never reflows when credits arrive, when a hold opens, or when runtime goes low. Numerals swap instantly and never tween.

An empty state is an invitation to act, not an apology for emptiness. An error says what happened and how to fix it — and never says sorry, because the person reading it wants the next step rather than a feeling.

What is not here

  • No dark theme. Adding one means a second block of raw values and a second set of pairs in contrast.test.ts — no component changes at all. Until those pairs are measured, dark would ship unmeasured.
  • No box-shadow, anywhere. Depth is the three luminance planes. A shadow would be a fourth mechanism for something three already do.
  • No motion except in answer to an action. No entrances, no shimmer, no counting numerals. The hold in particular is static: a moving hold would pull the eye continuously while someone is trying to work, which is the opposite of what a gauge on every screen is for.
  • No fourth state. Three is the whole vocabulary, and <Status>'s type makes a fourth unrepresentable on purpose.
